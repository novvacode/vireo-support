## The goal

**Cut delivery problems landing in Billing's queue from 34.9% of Billing's tickets to about 2.1%, by routing them on the customer's words instead of the bot's tag, worth about Rs 37,000 a quarter in avoided inter-team transfers (range Rs 23,000 to Rs 53,000).**

This is a **routing fix, not a hire**. At the base case it is worth Rs 1,46,347 a year, which is
16.3% of the Rs 9 lakh Finance quotes for two hires. It does not pay for two
hires; it removes a cost that hiring into Billing would not touch (section 4).

## 1. Baseline (measured, Jan-Jun 2026)

- **Window:** the most recent six months. All of it is on the new helpdesk, so `transfers` is known for every ticket.
- **Delivery work in Billing:** 314 of 900 Billing-tagged tickets (34.9%) were delivery work. Agent notes are the evidence (stage 2, F3).
- **Transfers, mis-tagged:** these averaged 0.904 transfers each.
- **Transfers, tagged right:** delivery tickets the bot sent straight to Logistics averaged 0.088.
- **Already handled in Billing:** 92 of the mis-tagged tickets were finished by Billing agents with no transfer. They are in the average, so they pull the saving *down*. That is correct: no transfer happened, so none can be saved.

## 2. Target and why it is justified

- **The target is not a chosen percentage.** It is what the categoriser already achieves when routing on the customer's message.
- **Recall:** against agents' own notes, which the tool never reads, it routes 96.1% of Billing-tagged delivery tickets to a delivery category (885/921, 95% CI 94.6% to 97.2%).
- **False positives:** it wrongly sends 1.33% of genuine billing tickets to delivery (20/1504).
- **What remains:** delivery work that would still land in Billing, as a share of the smaller Billing queue, is the target: 2.1%.
- **Independent support:** on AI-adjudicated gold samples the tool chose the right owner team for 96% of tickets, against 69%-79% for the bot (docs/validation.md). Those labels are AI-made, so they are supporting evidence, not the basis of the number.

### Why only transfers (no double counting)
A mis-tagged delivery ticket can lead to a transfer, a breach credit, a repeat contact, extra agent time and lower CSAT.
These are consequences of **one event**, so only one is monetised: the transfer.
- **Why the transfer:** the misroute causes it mechanically, it is recorded on every helpdesk ticket, and the policy prices it (Rs 305, "re-handling and administration").

**Excluded, deliberately:**
- **Breach credits (Rs 350).** Mis-tagged delivery tickets breach at 36.3% against 9.0% when tagged right. Priced as an *alternative* single method that is **not added**, it gives Rs 14,435 a quarter. It is excluded because the link is correlation, not mechanism, and it overlaps the transfer delay.
- **Agent-hours (Rs 165).** Re-handling time is already inside the Rs 305 transfer standard.
- **Repeat contacts.** No measurable excess: 5.2% for mis-tagged tickets against 5.3% for correctly tagged delivery (customer + category key, all 18 months).
- **Contact cost.** The first contact happens whichever team receives it.
- **CSAT.** Lower (2.35 vs 3.13), but no policy rupee value.
- **"Other"-tagged delivery work (stage 2 F16).** Not counted; mostly handled inside Frontline without a transfer.
- **The cost of making the change** (bot intake change or running the categoriser in the helpdesk). Not costed: no figure exists. The offline categoriser itself costs Rs 0 to run (section 6).

## 4. Against two hires, and Billing vs Logistics, in agent-hours

**Method.**
- **Workload:** policy-implied agent-hours, i.e. (contact cost + transfer cost) / Rs 165, per month, Jan-Jun 2026. Because the Rs 165 rate and the contact costs are both "fully loaded", these are cost-equivalent hours, not measured handle time.
- **Capacity:** agents x 8 h x 22 shifts (an assumption; the roster has no rota).
- **Two hires:** Rs 9 lakh / Rs 165 = 454.5 agent-hours a month.

| View | Team | Agents | Tickets / month | Workload hours / month | Available hours / month | Load | Load with +2 hires | Load if 650/week |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| bot tag | Billing | 4 | 150.0 | 341.0 | 704.0 | 48% | 32% | 173% |
| bot tag | Logistics | 5 | 122.7 | 228.4 | 880.0 | 26% | 19% | 93% |
| owner of the work | Billing | 4 | 97.7 | 182.0 | 704.0 | 26% | 17% | 93% |
| owner of the work | Logistics | 5 | 175.0 | 387.4 | 880.0 | 44% | 31% | 158% |

What this shows:
- **Hiring into Billing.** Counted by the bot's tag, Billing's load is 48%. Counted by the work it actually owns, it is 26%.
  - Two Billing hires would mostly add capacity for delivery work that Billing then transfers out anyway.
  - It would not remove the transfers or breach credits above, because those come from the routing, not from Billing being short-staffed.
- **Hiring into Logistics.** Counted by owner, Logistics carries the most per agent (44%, against Billing's 26%).
  - The routing fix moves about 50.3 tickets, or 68.7 agent-hours, a month into Logistics: 0.39 of one agent.
  - That is well under the 454.5 hours two hires would buy.
- **Cannot be concluded.** At the volume in this export, no team's policy-implied load exceeds 48% of the assumed available hours.
  - The data therefore **does not show that either team needs two hires**; ticket volume alone is not evidence of a staffing gap.
  - If the true volume is 650 a week (section 5), the last column applies. Counted by owner, Logistics would be over capacity (Logistics 158%) and Billing would not (Billing 93%). The hiring question would then point to Logistics.

## 7. What is measured, calculated, assumed
